\title{A Two-Transaction Counterexample to Repeated\\
One-Step Oracle Sampling in a Risk-Limiting Financial Audit}
\author{Richie Sater}
\date{August 11, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Shekhar, Xu, Lipton, Liang, and Ramdas derive a one-step oracle rule for
risk-limiting financial audits: sample a remaining transaction \(i\) with
probability proportional to its monetary misstatement contribution
\(\pi_i f_i\).  They do not claim that repeating this rule minimizes total
expected audit length.  We show that this derived global-optimality conjecture
is false for their released ApproxKelly initialization after intersection with
the logical confidence sequence.  An exact two-transaction instance has
expected stopping time \(3/2\) under the repeated oracle, whereas the
implementable proportional-to-recorded-value rule has expected stopping time
\(5/4\).  The calculation uses rational arithmetic, enumerates every terminal
history, and extends to an infinite rational family.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and the conjecture}

Consider \(N\) transactions with normalized recorded-value weights
\(\pi_i>0\), where \(\sum_i\pi_i=1\), and fixed unknown misstatement fractions
\(f_i\in[0,1]\).  The total monetary misstatement is
\[
  m^\star=\sum_{i=1}^N \pi_i f_i.
\]
At time \(t\), an auditor samples one remaining transaction from a predictable
distribution \(q_t\), observes its \(f_i\), updates a confidence sequence, and
stops when the confidence-set diameter is at most \(\varepsilon\).

Shekhar et al.\ \cite{shekhar2023} prove that, conditional on the current
history, the importance-weighted next observation has minimum variance under
\[
  q_t^{\mathrm{oracle}}(i)
  =\frac{\pi_i f_i}{\sum_{j\in R_t}\pi_j f_j}.
\]
This also maximizes a lower bound on one-step test-wealth growth.  They describe
the full fastest-uncertainty-reduction problem as a difficult multistage
optimization and solve the one-step surrogate instead.  The natural derived
conjecture is that repeating the displayed oracle rule minimizes
\(\E[\tau_\varepsilon]\) globally.  This conjecture, not a theorem or explicit
conjecture of \cite{shekhar2023}, is the target below.

\section{The pinned confidence construction}

For a candidate total \(m\), the paper's betting process has the update
\[
 W_t(m)=W_{t-1}(m)
 \left\{1+\lambda_t(m)\bigl(Z_t-\mu_t(m)\bigr)\right\},
 \qquad W_0(m)=1,
\]
and the betting confidence set retains candidates with \(W_t(m)<1/\delta\).
The logical interval after history \(I_1,\ldots,I_t\) is
\[
 [L_t,U_t]
 =\left[
 \sum_{s=1}^t\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s},
 \sum_{s=1}^t\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s}
 +\sum_{i\in R_{t+1}}\pi_i
 \right].
\]
We pin the construction to the authors' released implementation
\cite{implementation}: its ApproxKelly previous-payoff accumulator is
initialized at zero, its first bet is therefore \(\lambda_1=0\), and the
betting bounds are intersected with the logical interval and their running
intersection.  This is also the logical-CS refinement in equation (6) of
\cite{shekhar2023}.

\begin{lemma}[First-round reduction]
For the pinned construction and every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), the first-round
betting confidence set is \([0,1]\).  Consequently, after sampling \(I_1=i\),
the combined confidence-set diameter is \(\sum_{j\ne i}\pi_j\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\lambda_1=0\), one has \(W_1(m)=1\) for every \(m\in[0,1]\).  The
inequality \(\delta<1\) gives \(1<1/\delta\), so no candidate is rejected.
Intersecting \([0,1]\) with \([L_1,U_1]\) leaves the logical interval, whose
diameter is the sum of the remaining weights.
\end{proof}

\section{An exact counterexample}

\begin{theorem}
For the pinned construction, the repeated oracle policy does not minimize
expected audit length over randomized without-replacement policies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take \(N=2\), \(\varepsilon=1/3\), \(\delta=1/20\), and the following exact
population:
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}ccc}
\toprule
i & \pi_i & f_i & \pi_i f_i\\
\midrule
1 & 3/4 & 1/3 & 1/4\\
2 & 1/4 & 1   & 1/4\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
The oracle distribution at time one is therefore \((1/2,1/2)\).  Compare it
with the prop-M rule of \cite{shekhar2023}, which samples proportionally to
\(\pi_i\) and hence uses \((3/4,1/4)\).

If item 1 is sampled first, the first-round diameter is \(1/4\le 1/3\), so the
audit stops at time one.  If item 2 is sampled first, the diameter is
\(3/4>1/3\), so it does not stop.  After the other item is sampled at time two,
the logical interval is a singleton and the audit stops.  Thus a policy that
samples item 1 first with probability \(q\) has
\[
  \E[\tau]=q+2(1-q)=2-q.
\]
It follows that
\[
  \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{oracle}})]=2-\frac12=\frac32,
  \qquad
  \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{prop-M}})]=2-\frac34=\frac54.
\]
Since \(5/4<3/2\), prop-M is strictly better by \(1/4\) expected transaction.
\end{proof}

The mechanism is transparent.  The oracle makes the importance-weighted
monetary contribution constant, so it has zero one-step variance.  The logical
diameter, however, is controlled by remaining recorded-value weight.  On this
instance, variance minimization is indifferent between the two items while
logical-width reduction strongly favors item 1.

\section{An infinite family}

\begin{proposition}
Let \(p\in\Q\cap(1/2,1)\), and set
\[
 (\pi_1,\pi_2)=(p,1-p),
 \qquad
 (f_1,f_2)=\left(\frac{1-p}{p},1\right).
\]
Choose rational \(\varepsilon\) with \(1-p\le\varepsilon<p\), and choose any
rational \(\delta\in(0,1)\).  Then
\[
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{oracle}})]=\frac32,
 \qquad
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{prop-M}})]=2-p,
\]
so prop-M improves the expectation by \(p-1/2>0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both contributions equal \(1-p\), making the oracle uniform.  The condition on
\(\varepsilon\) says that sampling item 1 first stops the audit and sampling
item 2 first does not.  The first-round probabilities are \(1/2\) under the
oracle and \(p\) under prop-M.  Substitution in \(\E[\tau]=2-q\) proves the
claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and verification}

The theorem is deliberately narrow about the inferential construction.  It
does not address a betting CS without logical intersection, a different
first-round betting initialization, or the imperfect-information minimax
problem driven by estimated risk scores.  Nor does it assert that prop-M is
globally optimal.  It suffices to refute the universal claim for the released
construction.

The accompanying repository \cite{repository} represents every input and path
probability as a reduced rational number.  Its dynamic program enumerates the
two terminal histories for each policy and regenerates a JSON certificate.
No simulation, floating-point comparison, or numerical confidence-set grid is
used in the proof.
