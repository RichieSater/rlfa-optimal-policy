\author{Richie Sater}

\begin{abstract}
Risk-limiting financial audits estimate a weighted finite-population total by
adaptive sampling without replacement. Shekhar, Xu, Lipton, Liang, and Ramdas
derive a one-step oracle that samples transaction (i) proportionally to its
monetary misstatement contribution \(\pi_i f_i\), while leaving the multistage
optimal-policy problem open. We show that repeating this oracle can have the
worst possible finite-horizon approximation ratio throughout a broad class of
confidence sequences. For every \(N\ge2\), risk limit \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and
finite uniform betting-stake cap, there are rational weights, taints, and
tolerances having optimal expected audit length \(1\) and oracle expectation
\[
 1+\frac{N-1}{1+\rho},
\]
which approaches \(N\) as \(\rho\downarrow0\), for every predictable valid
non-control-variate betting strategy obeying the cap. The proof uses the
continuous confidence set and does not depend on ApproxKelly initialization or
a candidate grid. If review costs vary by a factor at most \(\kappa\), the
sharp worst-case cost-ratio supremum is \(1+(N-1)\kappa\); without a bound on
cost heterogeneity, no finite guarantee exists even for \(N=2\).

We then give a positive, implementable result for certified AI uncertainty.
If simultaneous intervals \(\ell_i\le f_i\le u_i\) are available, auditing in
descending order of \(\pi_i(u_i-\ell_i)\) is pathwise minimax-optimal for the
resulting robust certificate, for every finite population. We give the matching
lower bound, an exact heterogeneous-cost dynamic program, a valid risk-budget
composition with betting confidence sequences, exact finite-grid Bellman
verification, and reproducible synthetic stress tests. On one family, point
scores equal the realized taints yet randomized score sampling approaches both
sharp lower bounds, while valid simultaneous intervals identify a one-review,
unit-cost optimum. Prediction accuracy and certified audit value are therefore
mathematically different.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \(N\) transactions have normalized recorded-value weights \(\pi_i>0\),
\(\sum_i\pi_i=1\), and unknown misstatement fractions \(f_i\in[0,1]\). The
target is the total monetary misstatement
\[
 m^\star=\sum_{i=1}^N\pi_i f_i.
\]
At each round an auditor chooses a predictable distribution \(q_t\) on the
remaining transactions, observes one \(f_i\), updates an anytime-valid
confidence sequence, and stops when its diameter is at most \(\varepsilon\).
Shekhar et al.~\cite{shekhar2023} introduced this risk-limiting financial audit
(RLFA) framework. Their Proposition~2 minimizes the next importance-weighted
observation's variance and maximizes a lower bound on one-step wealth growth
using
\[
 q_t^{\rm oracle}(i)
 =\frac{\pi_i f_i}{\sum_{j\in R_t}\pi_j f_j}.             \tag{1}
\]
They explicitly describe the actual fastest-reduction problem as a difficult
multistage optimization and leave a fuller characterization for future work.
The issue is consequential: the confidence sequence also contains a logical
interval, whose width is the remaining recorded-value mass. Thus a draw can
have small local variance value but large terminal value, or vice versa.

This paper makes five contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
 \item We first solve the released construction exactly for \(N=2\), yielding
 a transparent counterexample to global oracle optimality.
 \item We prove a sharp arbitrary-\(N\) theorem for every risk limit and every
 uniformly bounded predictable betting strategy in the original
 non-control-variate class. The oracle's review-count ratio supremum is \(N\).
 \item We prove the exact heterogeneous-cost analogue: with cost ratio at most
 \(\kappa\), the sharp supremum is \(1+(N-1)\kappa\); without that restriction
 the ratio is unbounded already for two transactions.
 \item We solve an implementable certified-score problem. For simultaneous AI
 intervals, descending dollar-weighted interval width is globally optimal,
 a covering-knapsack dynamic program is exact for heterogeneous costs, and an
 exact family separates point accuracy from certified audit value.
 \item We provide an exact-rational Bellman solver on finite candidate and
 action grids, independent JSON certificates, exhaustive small-instance
 checks, and reproducible \(N=1000\) stress tests.
\end{enumerate}
The negative theorems concern stopping time and accumulated review cost; they
do not contradict Proposition~2. The bounded-betting result does not cover
unbounded stakes or arbitrary control-variate wealth processes. The positive
theorem is exact for a robust interval certificate; it does not purport to
solve the unrestricted continuous-action betting-CS control problem.

\section{Confidence construction and effort objective}

For a candidate total \(m\), the betting process updates
\[
 W_t(m)=W_{t-1}(m)
 \{1+\lambda_t(m)(Z_t-\mu_t(m))\},\qquad W_0(m)=1,       \tag{2}
\]
where
\[
 Z_t=\frac{\pi_{I_t}f_{I_t}}{q_t(I_t)},\qquad
 \mu_t(m)=m-\sum_{s<t}\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s}.
\]
The predictable stake must satisfy the usual constraint making each wealth
factor nonnegative. Candidates with \(W_t(m)\ge1/\delta\) are rejected. We
intersect the betting set with its running intersection and the logical interval
\[
 [L_t,U_t]=
 \left[
 \sum_{s\le t}\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s},
 \sum_{s\le t}\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s}+\sum_{i\in R_{t+1}}\pi_i
 \right].                                               \tag{3}
\]
Our main lower bound permits any valid predictable stake-selection strategy
for (2) having a finite uniform cap
\[
 \lvert\lambda_t(m)\rvert\le L<\infty                 \tag{4}
\]
at every history, time, and candidate. It uses the continuous theoretical set
of candidates. The released ApproxKelly implementation~\cite{implementation}
is a corollary: it initializes its previous-payoff array at zero, so
\(\lambda_1=0\), and caps \(\lvert\lambda_t(m)\rvert\le5/2\). The exact
two-item result below uses that zero first bet; the arbitrary-population result
does not.

The mathematical results use the exact denominator in \(Z_t\). Tiny
floating-point stabilizers in the research code are numerical implementation
details, not part of the martingale definition.

Definition~2 of \cite{shekhar2023} calls \(q_t\) a probability distribution on
the remaining set, and Proposition~2 optimizes over its simplex. We report
literal-simplex results and separately state what happens under strict full
support.

For known positive review costs \(c_i\), define accumulated manual effort by
\[
 C_\tau=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}c_{I_t},\qquad
 V_c^\star=\inf_q \E[C_\tau(q)].                       \tag{5}
\]
Unit costs recover expected audit length.
We call an audit nontrivial when its initial confidence-set diameter exceeds
\(\varepsilon\), so at least one review is required. The original construction
with initial set \([0,1]\) is nontrivial whenever \(\varepsilon<1\).

\section{The exact two-transaction problem}

The zero first bet makes the first-round betting set all of \([0,1]\). After
sampling item \(i\), the combined interval therefore has diameter
\(1-\pi_i\). Define
\[
 A(\pi,\varepsilon)=\{i\in\{1,2\}:1-\pi_i\le\varepsilon\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Complete \(N=2\) solution]
For every first action \(q\),
\[
\E_q[\tau]=2-\sum_{i\in A(\pi,\varepsilon)}q(i).        \tag{6}
\]
Hence the literal-simplex optimum is \(2\) if
\(\varepsilon<\min\{\pi_1,\pi_2\}\), and \(1\) otherwise. If
\(A\ne\varnothing\), the minimizers are exactly the distributions with
\(q(A)=1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first draw stops exactly on \(A\). Otherwise the sole remaining item is
audited at time two and (3) is a singleton. Thus
\(\E_q[\tau]=q(A)+2\{1-q(A)\}\), and optimizing gives the claim.
\end{proof}

For
\[
 (\pi_1,\pi_2)=\left(\frac34,\frac14\right),\quad
 (f_1,f_2)=\left(\frac13,1\right),\quad
 \varepsilon=\frac13,\quad\delta=\frac1{20},
\]
the optimum, prop-M, and oracle expectations are respectively
\[
 1<\frac54<\frac32.                                    \tag{7}
\]
Under strict full support the infimum remains one but is not attained; putting
probability \(1-\eta\) on item 1 gives expectation \(1+\eta\).

\section{Sharp bounds under bounded betting}

Fix \(N\ge2\), \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and a finite \(L\ge0\). Consider any
predictable valid non-control-variate betting strategy satisfying (4). Choose a
positive rational \(\varepsilon\) so that
\[
 \varepsilon<\frac13,
 \qquad (1+2L\varepsilon)^N<\frac1\delta.                \tag{8}
\]
Such a rational always exists. For any \(\rho\in(0,1]\), set
\[
 a=\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\qquad w=\frac{a}{N-1},         \tag{9}
\]
and define one large transaction and \(N-1\) small transactions by
\[
 \pi_0=1-a,\quad f_0=\frac{\rho w}{1-a};
 \qquad
 \pi_i=w,\quad f_i=1\quad(1\le i<N).                   \tag{10}
\]
The contributions are \(\rho w,w,\ldots,w\), all strictly positive.

\begin{lemma}[Two surviving continuous-set witnesses]
Until transaction \(0\) is audited, the combined running confidence set
contains
\[
 m_-=a,\qquad m_+=5a,                                   \tag{11}
\]
whose separation is \(2\varepsilon\), for every betting strategy in the stated
class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(k\) small transactions have been audited, the logical lower endpoint is
\(kw\le a\), while its upper endpoint is at least
\(\pi_0>5a\); the latter follows from \(\varepsilon<1/3\). Thus both witnesses
survive (3).

Under (1), write \(C_R\) for the remaining contribution and \(A\) for the
observed contribution. For every possible next item,
\[
 Z_t=\frac{\pi_i f_i}{\pi_i f_i/C_R}=C_R,
 \qquad Z_t-\mu_t(m)=m^\star-m,                          \tag{12}
\]
because \(C_R=m^\star-A\). Here \(m^\star=a+\rho w\), so at either witness
\(\lvert m^\star-m\rvert<2\varepsilon\). Every nonnegative wealth factor is
therefore at most \(1+2L\varepsilon\), regardless of how the predictable stake
was initialized or selected. For all \(t\le N\),
\[
 W_t(m_\pm)\le(1+2L\varepsilon)^t
 \le(1+2L\varepsilon)^N<\frac1\delta.                   \tag{13}
\]
Neither witness is rejected. Since both survive at every preceding time, the
running intersection retains both.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp bounded-betting review-count ratio]
For (9)--(10),
\[
 V^\star=1,\qquad
 \E[\tau_{\rm oracle}]
 =1+\frac{N-1}{1+\rho}.                                 \tag{14}
\]
For every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\), finite cap \(L\), and bounded predictable
strategy in the stated class, the worst-case oracle-to-optimal expected-review
ratio supremum over \(N\)-transaction instances is exactly \(N\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Before item \(0\) is sampled, the lemma makes the combined diameter at least
\(2\varepsilon>\varepsilon\). Once item \(0\) is sampled, the remaining
recorded-value weight is at most \(a\le\varepsilon\), so (3) stops the audit.
Thus \(\tau_{\rm oracle}\) is exactly the rank of item \(0\).

Sequential probability-proportional sampling is the order of independent
exponential clocks with the corresponding rates. Each small rate-\(w\) clock
precedes the rate-\(\rho w\) clock with probability \(1/(1+\rho)\). Linearity
of expectation gives (14). Auditing item \(0\) first stops in one round by the
logical interval, for every betting strategy under consideration. Finally,
every nontrivial audit uses at least one and at most \(N\) reviews, while (14)
approaches \(N\) as \(\rho\downarrow0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp heterogeneous-cost ratio]
Fix \(\kappa\ge1\). Among nontrivial \(N\)-transaction audits in the preceding
bounded-betting class with
\(\max_i c_i/\min_i c_i\le\kappa\), the worst-case oracle-to-optimal expected
review-cost ratio supremum is
\[
 \boxed{1+(N-1)\kappa}.                                 \tag{15}
\]
Without a bound on cost heterogeneity, no finite approximation guarantee exists,
even for \(N=2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In (10), assign \(c_0=1\) and \(c_i=\kappa\) for \(i\ge1\). The audit stops
exactly when item \(0\) appears, and each small item precedes it with probability
\(1/(1+\rho)\). Hence
\[
 V_c^\star=1,
 \qquad
 \E[C_{\rm oracle}]
 =1+\frac{(N-1)\kappa}{1+\rho},                         \tag{16}
\]
which approaches (15) as \(\rho\downarrow0\).

For the matching upper bound, normalize \(\min_i c_i=1\). A complete audit
costs at most \(\sum_i c_i\le1+(N-1)\kappa\), while every nontrivial audit
costs at least one. If heterogeneity is unrestricted, the \(N=2\) expression
\(1+\kappa/(1+\rho)\) diverges with \(\kappa\) for any fixed \(\rho>0\).
\end{proof}

Every oracle action has strict full support when \(\rho>0\). If competitors
must also have strict support, the values one in (14) and (16) are unattained
infima: put first-round mass \(1-\eta\) on item \(0\), use any fully supported
continuation, and let \(\eta\downarrow0\).

\paragraph{Released ApproxKelly corollary.}
Set \(\delta=1/20\), \(L=5/2\), and
\(\varepsilon=1/(20N)\). Then
\[
 (1+2L\varepsilon)^N
 =\left(1+\frac1{4N}\right)^N<e^{1/4}<2<20.
\]
The two witnesses in (11) also lie at indices 1 and 5 of the allowed uniform
grid with \(40N+1\) points. Thus the released ApproxKelly construction is a
corollary, but neither its zero initialization nor its grid drives the general
theorem.

Prop-M stops no later than the rank of item \(0\), whose expectation is
\[
 1+\frac{(N-1)w}{\pi_0+w}
 =1+\frac{a}{\pi_0+w}<\frac1{\pi_0},                   \tag{17}
\]
which approaches one. If point scores happen to equal the realized taints,
\(S_i=f_i\), prop-MS is identical to (1) and inherits both sharp lower bounds.
This is a statement about the constructed scores and outcomes, not a claim that
the point model has a certified zero-error guarantee.

\section{Certified AI intervals: an exact minimax policy}

The negative theorems say that point contributions do not encode terminal
uncertainty. Suppose instead that a model, prior audit, or calibrated procedure
supplies simultaneous bounds
\[
 \ell_i\le f_i\le u_i\qquad(1\le i\le N).              \tag{18}
\]
After auditing a set \(B\), the exact image of this box under the total is
\[
 C_B^{\rm box}=
 \left[
 \sum_{i\in B}\pi_i f_i+\sum_{i\notin B}\pi_i\ell_i,
 \sum_{i\in B}\pi_i f_i+\sum_{i\notin B}\pi_i u_i
 \right].                                               \tag{19}
\]
Both endpoints are attainable because the uncertainty set is a Cartesian
product. Define the dollar-weighted uncertainty
\[
 d_i=\pi_i(u_i-\ell_i),\qquad
 D(B)=\sum_{i\notin B}d_i.                              \tag{20}
\]
The observation changes the location of (19), but not its width \(D(B)\).

\begin{theorem}[Pathwise minimax box policy]
Let \(d_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge d_{(N)}\), and define
\[
 k^\star=\min\left\{k\in\{0,\ldots,N\}:
 \sum_{r=k+1}^N d_{(r)}\le\varepsilon\right\}.         \tag{21}
\]
Auditing in nonincreasing order of \(d_i\) stops (19) in exactly
\(k^\star\) reviews. Moreover, over all adaptive randomized policies,
\[
 \inf_q\sup_{f\in\prod_i[\ell_i,u_i]}
 \E_f[\tau_{\rm box}(q)]=k^\star.                      \tag{22}
\]
The lower bound holds on every sample path.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The sorted policy leaves the residual in (21). Any history of length
\(t<k^\star\) can remove at most \(\sum_{r=1}^t d_{(r)}\). Its residual width
therefore exceeds \(\varepsilon\), regardless of observations, randomization,
or adaptation. No policy can stop earlier on any path, while the sorted
construction attains the bound.
\end{proof}

This gives a precise answer to the implementable robust-certificate problem:
audit uncertainty, not merely predicted error. For a multiplicative guarantee,
write the condition without division as
\[
 (1-b)f_i\le S_i\le(1+b)f_i,\qquad 0\le b<1.            \tag{23}
\]
It implies
\[
 \ell_i=\frac{S_i}{1+b},\qquad
 u_i=\min\left\{1,\frac{S_i}{1-b}\right\}.             \tag{24}
\]
If no upper endpoint clips, then
\[
 d_i=\pi_i S_i\frac{2b}{1-b^2}.                        \tag{25}
\]
Thus \(\pi_iS_i\) is the correct priority, but it should be used as a
descending deterministic priority for this certificate, not as a randomized
probability. When (24) clips, the exact priority in (20) can differ even in
rank from \(\pi_iS_i\).

\begin{corollary}[Point accuracy versus certified audit value]
On the population (9)--(10), let the point scores happen to equal the realized
taints, \(S_i=f_i\), and supply the simultaneous intervals
\[
 [\ell_0,u_0]=\left[0,\frac{2\varepsilon}{\pi_0}\right],
 \qquad [\ell_i,u_i]=[0,1]\quad(1\le i<N).             \tag{26}
\]
The certified policy stops in one review. Randomized prop-MS has
\[
 \E[\tau_{\rm propMS}]=1+\frac{N-1}{1+\rho}.
\]
With \(c_0=1\) and \(c_i=\kappa\), its expected review cost is (16), while the
certified cost optimum is one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The large upper endpoint is below one because \(\varepsilon<1/3\). The
intervals contain the realized taints: in particular,
\(\pi_0f_0=\rho w\le2\varepsilon\). Their uncertainty contributions satisfy
\[
 d_0=2\varepsilon,
 \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{N-1}d_i=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}w
 =a=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
\]
No audited set omitting item \(0\) can stop, because its residual contains
\(d_0>\varepsilon\). Auditing item \(0\) leaves residual
\(a\le\varepsilon\), so descending uncertainty stops after one review. Since
\(S=f\), prop-MS uses the oracle rates and stops exactly at item \(0\)'s rank.
The review and cost formulas follow from the exponential-clock calculation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Heterogeneous costs}

For general positive \(c_i\), the exact robust problem becomes
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \min_{x\in\{0,1\}^N}\quad&\sum_i c_i x_i\\
 \text{s.t.}\quad&\sum_i d_i x_i
 \ge D(\varnothing)-\varepsilon.
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{27}
\]
The repository implements an exact rational Pareto-frontier dynamic program.
After each item, a state records removed width and cost, caps width at the
target, and discards a state if another has at least as much removed width at
no greater cost. Induction over items proves that every discarded partial
solution is dominated and that the least-cost terminal state solves (27).

\subsection{Risk accounting and a hybrid audit}

Suppose (18) holds simultaneously with probability at least
\(1-\delta_{\rm AI}\). On that event, (19) covers \(m^\star\) at every time,
under any adaptive policy. If a betting CS has simultaneous failure probability
\(\delta_{\rm CS}\), their running intersection fails with probability at most
\[
 \delta_{\rm AI}+\delta_{\rm CS}                        \tag{28}
\]
by a union bound; independence is unnecessary. A deployable architecture can
therefore individually examine a certainty stratum chosen by (20), then run a
randomized risk-limiting procedure on the residual population, explicitly
allocating the two risk budgets.

PCAOB AS~2315 describes one sample as more efficient when it achieves the same
objectives with a smaller sample, calls for consideration of relative cost and
effectiveness when choosing a sampling approach, and distinguishes individually
examined items from the sampled residual population~\cite{pcaob2315}.
Representative residual samples must still give population items an opportunity
for selection. This is operational context, not a legal or professional-
compliance conclusion.

\section{Finite-grid Bellman verification}

For general \(N\), the state must contain the ordered history, remaining set,
wealth at every candidate \(m\), ApproxKelly payoff accumulators, and current
running intersection. If \(\Phi(x,q,i)\) is the transition, the exact equation
is
\[
 V(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&x\text{ is in the stopping region},\\
 1+\displaystyle\inf_q\sum_{i\in R(x)}q(i)V(\Phi(x,q,i)),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                            \tag{29}
\]
The action changes both transition probability and confidence state. This is
the continuation value absent from the one-step surrogate.

We implement (29) with exact rational arithmetic on a finite candidate grid
and the strict-support mesh \(q_i\in\{1/D,\ldots,D/D\}\). Backward induction
over the finite horizon gives the global optimum over that finite action set;
no simulation enters these values. Table~\ref{tab:small} uses a 21-point
candidate grid and \(D=6\). Policies not lying on this action mesh can beat its
optimum, so the table is verification and structure discovery, not a
continuous-action optimality claim.

\section{Related work and novelty boundary}

Waudby-Smith and Ramdas~\cite{waudby2020} developed confidence sequences for
sampling without replacement; Shekhar et al.~\cite{shekhar2023} extended this
direction to adaptively weighted monetary totals and AI side information.
Imberg et al.~\cite{imberg2024} study machine-learning-assisted active sampling
for finite-population inference. Their optimal importance probabilities use
predicted means and covariances to minimize expected asymptotic MSE. That work
reinforces the importance of predictive uncertainty, but it does not optimize
an RLFA stopping time or the exact robust certificate (19).

Kato and Nakagawa~\cite{kato2026} study sequential audit sampling with exact
finite-population boundary conditions and expected stopping times. Their model
is binary deviation-rate auditing under a hypergeometric random order; they
explicitly place monetary-unit sampling outside its direct scope. It neither
resolves adaptive weighted selection nor the oracle question here.

Active sequential hypothesis testing considers controlled information
acquisition~\cite{naghshvar2012}, including nonuniform observation
costs~\cite{vershinin2025}.
Those models repeatedly select noisy experiments to decide among hypotheses
and establish dynamic-programming or asymptotic guarantees; they do not give
the exact finite-population RLFA ratios here. Goerigk et al.~\cite{goerigk2015}
study queries under interval uncertainty for robust knapsack, but with a
different packing objective and query budget.

Three ten-angle literature searches, including exact-title citation checks,
cost-sensitive sequential design, and 2024--2026 work, found no prior statement
of the bounded-betting theorem, sharp review-cost ratio, or exact box policy.
This is not a novelty guarantee. Before submission, the result should be checked in
subscription indexes, sent to the original authors, and independently reviewed
by sequential-analysis and audit-methodology experts.

\section{Discussion}

The sharp lower bound identifies the control-theoretic failure precisely: zero
next-step importance variance does not imply fast entry into a stopping region.
In the constructed family, a nearly risk-free but very large recorded value has
tiny \(\pi_i f_i\), so the oracle postpones the one transaction whose review
ends the audit.

The cost theorem turns this from a sample-count pathology into an effort
guarantee. With bounded heterogeneity the oracle attains the largest ratio any
without-replacement audit can have; without such a bound its ratio is
unbounded. The obstruction is again terminal value, not inaccurate point
prediction.

Certified intervals turn that diagnosis into an exact implementable policy.
They expose how much each unaudited transaction can still move the total. Under
independent coordinate bounds this uncertainty is modular, which makes
descending \(\pi_i(u_i-\ell_i)\) pathwise optimal. The result is deliberately
conditional on simultaneous calibration. A point model with good average
accuracy but no joint coverage guarantee cannot be inserted into (19) while
preserving audit risk.

The calibration separation makes the distinction exact on one population.
Point scores can equal every realized taint and still induce nearly maximal
work when randomized by predicted contribution. Simultaneous intervals over
the same transactions encode a different object---remaining certifiable dollar
uncertainty---and recover the exact work plan.

Unbounded betting, arbitrary control variates, the continuous-action solution
of (29), correlated uncertainty, and field-data validation remain separate
problems. They are not needed for the stated sharp results. The immediate next
step is adversarial mathematical and audit-methodology review, not expansion of
the theorem surface.
