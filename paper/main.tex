\title{Exact Two-Transaction Sampling Policies for\\
Risk-Limiting Financial Audits}
\author{Richie Sater}
\date{August 11, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Shekhar, Xu, Lipton, Liang, and Ramdas derive a one-step oracle rule for
risk-limiting financial audits: sample a remaining transaction \(i\) with
probability proportional to its monetary misstatement contribution
\(\pi_i f_i\). They do not claim that repeating this rule minimizes total
expected audit length. For their released ApproxKelly initialization
intersected with the logical confidence sequence, we solve the global sampling
problem exactly when \(N=2\). For every first-stage distribution \(q\),
\[
 \E_q[\tau]
 =2-\sum_{i:\,1-\pi_i\le\varepsilon}q(i).
\]
Thus the optimum is \(1\) if
\(\varepsilon\ge\min\{\pi_1,\pi_2\}\), and \(2\) otherwise. An exact rational
instance has expected stopping times \(1\), \(5/4\), and \(3/2\) under the
deterministic optimum, prop-M, and the repeated oracle, respectively. We also
characterize strict-support conventions and give an infinite rational family.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and the one-step oracle}

Consider \(N\) transactions with normalized recorded-value weights
\(\pi_i>0\), where \(\sum_i\pi_i=1\), and fixed misstatement fractions
\(f_i\in[0,1]\). The total monetary misstatement is
\[
  m^\star=\sum_{i=1}^N \pi_i f_i.
\]
At time \(t\), an auditor samples one remaining transaction from a predictable
distribution \(q_t\), observes its \(f_i\), updates a confidence sequence, and
stops when the confidence-set diameter is at most \(\varepsilon\).

Shekhar et al.\ \cite{shekhar2023} prove that, conditional on the current
history, the importance-weighted next observation has minimum variance under
\[
  q_t^{\mathrm{oracle}}(i)
  =\frac{\pi_i f_i}{\sum_{j\in R_t}\pi_j f_j}.
\]
This rule also maximizes a lower bound on one-step test-wealth growth. The
paper describes the full fastest-uncertainty-reduction problem as a difficult
multistage optimization and solves the one-step surrogate instead. We study
the natural derived question of whether repeating this oracle minimizes
\(\E[\tau]\). This question is not stated as a conjecture in
\cite{shekhar2023}.

\section{The pinned confidence construction}

For a candidate total \(m\), the paper's betting process has update
\[
 W_t(m)=W_{t-1}(m)
 \left\{1+\lambda_t(m)\bigl(Z_t-\mu_t(m)\bigr)\right\},
 \qquad W_0(m)=1,
\]
and retains candidates with \(W_t(m)<1/\delta\). The logical interval after
history \(I_1,\ldots,I_t\) is
\[
 [L_t,U_t]
 =\left[
 \sum_{s=1}^t\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s},
 \sum_{s=1}^t\pi_{I_s}f_{I_s}
 +\sum_{i\in R_{t+1}}\pi_i
 \right].
\]
We pin the construction to the authors' released implementation
\cite{implementation}: the ApproxKelly previous-payoff accumulator is
initialized at zero, its first bet is \(\lambda_1=0\), and the betting bounds
are intersected with the logical interval and their running intersection. The
logical refinement is equation (6) of \cite{shekhar2023}.

\begin{lemma}[First-round reduction]
For every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), the first-round betting confidence set in the
pinned construction is \([0,1]\). After sampling \(I_1=i\), the combined
confidence-set diameter is \(1-\pi_i\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\lambda_1=0\), one has \(W_1(m)=1\) for every \(m\in[0,1]\).
The inequality \(\delta<1\) gives \(1<1/\delta\), so the betting process rejects
no candidate. Intersecting with the logical interval leaves an interval whose
diameter is the total remaining weight, namely \(1-\pi_i\).
\end{proof}

\section{Admissible actions and support}

Definition 2 of \cite{shekhar2023} defines \(q_t\) as a probability
distribution on the remaining set. Proposition 2 optimizes over the simplex
of such distributions, and Remark 5 discusses deterministic sampling. The
literal action space therefore includes boundary distributions.

There is nevertheless a support subtlety. The generic identity
\[
 \E\!\left[\frac{\pi_{I_t}f_{I_t}}{q_t(I_t)}
 \,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]
 =\sum_{i\in R_t}\pi_i f_i
\]
requires positive mass on each item with positive contribution. We therefore
state results both for the literal simplex and for conservative strict-support
conventions. In the deterministic \(N=2\) optimum below, the first bet is zero
and the audit stops from the logical interval alone, so no later
importance-weighted bet is used.

\pagebreak
\section{\texorpdfstring{Exact solution for \(N=2\)}{Exact solution for N=2}}

For positive \(\pi_1+\pi_2=1\), define the first-round stopping set
\[
 A(\pi,\varepsilon)
 :=\{i\in\{1,2\}:1-\pi_i\le\varepsilon\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Complete \(N=2\) characterization]
Fix any \(f_1,f_2\in[0,1]\), \(\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)\), and the pinned
construction. For every first-stage distribution \(q\in\DeltaTwo\),
\[
 \E_q[\tau]
 =2-\sum_{i\in A(\pi,\varepsilon)}q(i).                 \tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 V^\star
 :=\min_{q\in\DeltaTwo}\E_q[\tau]
 =
 \begin{cases}
 2,&\varepsilon<\min\{\pi_1,\pi_2\},\\
 1,&\varepsilon\ge\min\{\pi_1,\pi_2\}.
 \end{cases}                                           \tag{2}
\]
If \(A\) is empty, every \(q\) is optimal. If \(A\) is nonempty, the
minimizers are exactly the distributions satisfying \(q(A)=1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the lemma, sampling item \(i\) first stops the audit exactly when \(i\in A\).
This event has probability \(q(A)\). On its complement, the only remaining
item is sampled at time two, when the logical interval is a singleton. Hence
\[
 \E_q[\tau]=q(A)+2\{1-q(A)\}=2-q(A),
\]
which proves (1). A distribution can make \(q(A)=1\) exactly when \(A\) is
nonempty. Finally, \(A\ne\varnothing\) if and only if
\(\varepsilon\ge\min\{\pi_1,\pi_2\}\). This gives (2) and the optimizer set.
\end{proof}

The optimal policy is independent of both \(f\) and \(\delta\). In the
one-stopping-item regime, it audits the larger-weight transaction
deterministically.

\begin{corollary}[Oracle-optimality criterion]
Assume \(m^\star>0\), so the proportional oracle is defined. If \(A\) is empty
or \(A=\{1,2\}\), the oracle is globally optimal because every policy is
optimal. If \(A=\{k\}\) and \(j\ne k\), then
\[
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{oracle}})]
 =2-\frac{\pi_kf_k}{\pi_1f_1+\pi_2f_2}.
\]
It equals the literal-simplex optimum \(1\) exactly when
\(\pi_jf_j=0\). If both contributions are positive, the oracle's exact gap is
\[
 \frac{\pi_jf_j}{\pi_1f_1+\pi_2f_2}>0.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the oracle distribution in (1). In the singleton regime the
result equals \(1\) precisely when the oracle assigns probability one to
\(k\), equivalently when the other contribution vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strict-support conventions]
If \(A\) is a singleton, the infimum over strictly positive distributions is
\(1\), but it is not attained. The family assigning probabilities
\((1-\eta,\eta)\) to the stopping and nonstopping items has expectation
\(1+\eta\). If support is required only on positive-contribution items, the
infimum is attained exactly when the nonstopping contribution is zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation (1) is strictly decreasing in the nonstopping probability. Strict
positivity forces that probability to be positive but permits it to approach
zero. Under contribution support it may equal zero exactly when the
nonstopping contribution is zero.
\end{proof}

\section{A certified counterexample}

Take \(N=2\), \(\varepsilon=1/3\), \(\delta=1/20\), and
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}ccc}
\toprule
i & \pi_i & f_i & \pi_i f_i\\
\midrule
1 & 3/4 & 1/3 & 1/4\\
2 & 1/4 & 1   & 1/4\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
Here \(A=\{1\}\). The literal-simplex optimum audits item 1 with probability
one and stops with certainty. The oracle is uniform because the contributions
are equal, while prop-M samples item 1 with probability \(3/4\). Equation (1)
gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{opt}})]=1
 <\E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{prop-M}})]=\frac54
 <\E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{oracle}})]=\frac32.}
\]
The comparison \(5/4<3/2\) uses two fully supported policies and therefore
does not depend on the action-space ambiguity. Under strict full support,
\(q_\eta=(1-\eta,\eta)\) further improves prop-M whenever
\(\eta<1/4\), and approaches the infimum \(1\).

\section{An infinite rational family}

Let \(p\in\Q\cap(1/2,1)\), and set
\[
 (\pi_1,\pi_2)=(p,1-p),
 \qquad
 (f_1,f_2)=\left(\frac{1-p}{p},1\right).
\]
Choose rational \(\varepsilon\) with \(1-p\le\varepsilon<p\), and any rational
\(\delta\in(0,1)\). Both contributions equal \(1-p\), so the oracle is
uniform and \(A=\{1\}\). The theorem gives
\[
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{opt}})]=1,\qquad
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{prop-M}})]=2-p,\qquad
 \E[\tau(q^{\mathrm{oracle}})]=\frac32.
\]
Thus the oracle gap from the true literal-simplex optimum is \(1/2\) throughout
the family, while its gap from prop-M is \(p-1/2\).

\section{Bellman formulation and verification}

For \(N\ge3\), a state must record the ordered history, remaining set, wealth
function, ApproxKelly accumulators, and running logical/probabilistic
intersection. If \(\Phi(x,q,i)\) is the next state after action \(q\) draws
item \(i\), and \(D\) is the stopping region, the remaining-cost Bellman
equation is
\[
 V(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&x\in D,\\
 1+\displaystyle\inf_q\sum_{i\in R(x)}q(i)V(\Phi(x,q,i)),&x\notin D.
 \end{cases}
\]
The dependence of \(\Phi\) on \(q\) through importance weighting is essential.
Proposition 2 optimizes a local variance term, not the displayed continuation
value.

The accompanying repository \cite{repository} represents every parameter and
path probability as a reduced rational number. It enumerates all terminal
histories, verifies all three support conventions, and independently
regenerates a JSON certificate. No simulation, floating-point comparison, or
numerical confidence-set grid is used.

The result is deliberately pinned to the released first-round initialization
and logical intersection. It does not solve \(N\ge3\), a betting CS without
the logical intersection, or the imperfect-information minimax problem.
